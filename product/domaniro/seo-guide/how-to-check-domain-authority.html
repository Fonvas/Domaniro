How to Check a Website's Domain Authority Without Moz or a Paid Tool

You don't need a Moz account, a credit card, or a monthly quota to get a fast read on how strong a domain is. Here's what the metric actually measures, and how to check it for free.

If you've spent any time in SEO, link building, or content marketing, you've run into "Domain Authority" — a score, usually from 1 to 100, meant to estimate how likely a website is to rank in search results. It's become shorthand for "is this site worth my time," whether you're vetting a guest post opportunity, sizing up a competitor, or deciding if a backlink is actually valuable.

Here's the catch: Domain Authority is a trademarked, proprietary metric owned by Moz, and checking it officially requires a Moz account — free accounts come with tight limits, and full access sits behind a subscription. That's a lot of friction for what's often a quick gut-check on a domain.

What the score is actually trying to tell you

Strip away the branding, and every "authority" metric — Moz's DA, Ahrefs' DR, Semrush's Authority Score, or an independent one — is trying to answer the same underlying question: how many other sites, and how credible are they, that link to this domain? A site with links from thousands of unique, established domains tends to score high. A brand-new site with a handful of low-quality backlinks scores low. None of these scores measure ranking directly — they're a proxy, a fast way to estimate a domain's general "weight" without doing a full link audit by hand.

That's important context: no authority score, from any provider, is an exact or official ranking factor. They're all estimates, computed differently, and they won't agree with each other perfectly. What matters is having a consistent number to compare domains against each other quickly — not chasing a specific provider's exact figure.

A quick note on comparing scores across tools

If you check the same domain in Domaniro, Moz, and Ahrefs, expect three different numbers. That's normal and not a sign anything is "wrong" — each provider crawls a different slice of the web and weighs links differently. Use whichever score you check consistently to compare domains against each other, rather than treating any single number as an absolute, universal truth.
